\section{Judge Model Robustness Check: gpt-4o vs.\ gpt-5.6-sol}
\label{sec:judge-model-comparison}

Section~\ref{sec:llm-baseline} scores every baseline-vs-director comparison
with gpt-4o as the judge. To check whether that result is an artifact of
the specific judge model, the three Red Riding Hood derailment levels
(none, medium, complete; Olaf, baseline vs.\ director) were rejudged from
scratch with a different model, gpt-5.6-sol, using the identical prompts,
rubric, and order-bias-controlled AB/BA protocol described in
Section~\ref{sec:llm-judge-method} -- only the judge model changes. Winner
cells where the two judges disagree are marked in \textbf{bold}.

\subsection{Reading}

Overall winner agrees in all 3 scenarios (director wins under both judges,
every time) -- the headline result is judge-robust. Every disagreement at
the individual-dimension level, except one, is gpt-4o calling a dimension
"Same" where gpt-5.6-sol calls it an outright director win; the single
exception (Narrative Control, complete derailment) runs the other way,
gpt-4o says director wins, gpt-5.6-sol calls it a tie. gpt-5.6-sol's
absolute scores also run consistently lower than gpt-4o's across both
sides of every comparison -- it reads as the stricter scorer of the two,
not merely a different one.

This does \emph{not} hold for the derailment-severity trend reported in
Section~\ref{sec:llm-baseline}: under gpt-4o, director's Overall margin over
baseline shrinks as derailment intensifies (2.0 $\to$ 1.0 $\to$ 1.0); under
gpt-5.6-sol it widens instead (2.0 $\to$ 3.5 $\to$ 4.7). Both judges agree
director always wins -- they disagree on whether that advantage is most
visible when the story is calm or when it is under the heaviest pressure.
With n=3 scenarios this is a spot check, not a replication, but it is
reason enough to caveat the severity-trend claim as judge-dependent rather
than treating it as an established property of the system.
