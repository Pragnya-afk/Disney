What the Wilcoxon signed-rank test is telling you

This is a paired, non-parametric test — the right tool here because (a) every participant rated both conditions, so the natural comparison is each person's own director-minus-baseline difference, not two independent groups, and (b) Likert ratings (1–5) are ordinal, not interval-scaled, so a paired t-test's normality/equal-interval assumptions don't hold.

Mechanically: for each of the 14 participants, it takes (director rating − baseline rating), ranks those differences by absolute size, and asks whether the signed ranks are skewed toward one direction more than chance would produce. A pair where the two ratings are equal contributes nothing and is dropped — that's why n drops from 14 to 11 for "resisted derailment" (three participants left that field blank in one of the two conditions, not because they rated it a tie).

  • p = 0.001 (coherence), p = 0.002 (resisted derailment): it would be very unlikely (≈0.1–0.2% chance) to see this consistent a director > baseline pattern across participants if the two conditions were really equivalent. Combined with the size of the mean gap (3.93 vs 1.71, and 4.36 vs 1.55 — roughly 2 points on a 5-point scale), this is a genuine, strong, individually-consistent effect: it's not just the average that moved, most people personally rated director higher than baseline on these two measures.
  • p = 0.036 (engagement): a weaker but still-present signal — some but not all participants rated director as more engaging than baseline. Worth reporting but shouldn't be leaned on as hard as coherence/resistance.
  • p = 0.12–0.93 everywhere else (character consistency in story 1; every measure in story 2 director-vs-time_constrained): no evidence of a real within-subject difference — the ranked differences look consistent with random scatter around zero. For story 2 specifically, that's the intended finding: cutting the director's time budget to 5 minutes didn't measurably change how participants rated it on any measure.
  • What the test does not tell you: effect size or direction magnitude — that's why every p-value above is reported next to the actual means, not alone. It also doesn't adjust for multiple comparisons (10 tests run across the two tables) — with no correction applied, treat the p=0.036 result as suggestive rather than confirmatory, while the two p<0.005 results are robust to any reasonable correction.

Takeaway: director mode is significantly and consistently better than baseline on coherence and derailment-resistance (and, more weakly, on engagement); character consistency is high and statistically indistinguishable across all three conditions; imposing a 5-minute time limit on the director doesn't measurably hurt any measure (all p > 0.39).

(time_constrained's n=15 isn't a typo for the 14 study-completers: it's those 14, each contributing one time_constrained response, plus z7-S4ed7 — who fully completed phases 1–3, including a submitted time_constrained questionnaire in phase 3, but then never engaged with phase 4 at all, so they don't count as a study "completer" even though this particular phase response of theirs is real and complete.)

How the director beats-completed numbers were derived. beats_completed is a self-report from the director LLM (should_complete_beat in its JSON output each turn) with no code-level override, and it turns out to dramatically under-report: it sat at 0 for the large majority of director phases even when the transcript's actual narration had clearly walked through several beats' worth of plot (see the worked example below). To get a real number, every one of the 39 director-condition phases in outputs_all was manually read against its scenario's beat list (5 beats for Frozen, 9 for Cinderella — defined in Implementation/scenarios/olaf_derailment_scenario_suite.py) and beats_completed/completed_beats were corrected directly in the source JSON files to reflect which beats' core events the narration actually depicted, in order. baseline's numbers are not recoded — they're still the original, equally-unreliable auto-logged values — because the task scope was director-only; treat baseline's 0.35 as an instrumentation artifact, not evidence of low progress.

Worked example: outputs_all/phase_4/session_20260723_082157_HgvNXiBO.json, phase 4 (director, Cinderella) — 28 turns, ending in a submitted questionnaire — was logged as beats_completed: 0. Reading the transcript: turn 4 covers the stepsisters' chore list (beat 2), turn 8 the invitation arriving (beat 3), turn 12 the fairy godmother appearing (beat 4), turns 15–16 the transformation and midnight warning (beat 5), turns 17–19 the ball and meeting the prince (beat 6), turn 20 the midnight flight and lost slipper (beat 7), turns 21–27 the kingdom-wide slipper search (beat 8), and turn 28 the slipper fitting Cinderella (beat 9) — every single beat, back to back, narrated correctly. Corrected value: beats_completed: 9 (maximum possible). This is the most extreme case, but the direction (auto-log under-counts, sometimes severely) held throughout: of the 33 questionnaire-completed director phases, 19 needed correction, all upward, several from 0 or 1 to 2–4 (one to 9).

Turns-per-phase never had this problem and remains the safer unadjusted proxy for conversation length across all three conditions — by that measure, all three produce comparably long conversations (~9–12 turns on average among completed phases), with baseline slightly ahead of director and director showing the highest spread (SD 4.56 vs baseline's 3.28). With the director beats-completed numbers now corrected, director still trails time_constrained by a wide margin (2.24 vs 8.13 of 9 beats) — but that gap is expected and not a quality signal: time_constrained is explicitly force-advanced toward the story's end as its 5-minute budget runs out (server.py's critical/final pacing modes), while unconstrained director rounds are cut off by a fixed 5-minute timer or a manual "Finish Story" click with no such forcing, so a shorter fraction of the (longer, less rushed) story typically gets covered before time runs out.

5. Files in this folder

  • build_dataset.py — builds phases.csv / participants.csv from ../outputs_all/phase_*/*.json, deduplicating participant restarts (keeps the latest attempt).
  • run_stats.py — all statistical tests above; writes stats_report.txt.
  • make_plots.py — writes plots/01–08 (retention, dropout-by-condition, all-responses-by-condition, turns-by-condition boxplot, completers-all-measures, story comparison, beats-completed-by-condition, turns-mean-SD-by-condition).
  • To regenerate after new sessions land in outputs_all/: run build_dataset.py, then run_stats.py, then make_plots.py, in that order, with a Python environment that has pandas/numpy/scipy/matplotlib (e.g. /home/prachakonda/Disney/.venv).

Provenance note on outputs_all/: beats_completed / completed_beats for all 39 director-condition phases were manually recoded in place (see §3) by reading each transcript against its scenario's beat list and correcting the auto-logged (self-reported, unreliable) values directly in the source JSON files — this is a real edit to the raw study data, not a derived/separate field. A full pre-edit backup of outputs_all/ was taken first, at ../outputs_all_backup_pre_beat_recode_<timestamp>/, so the original auto-logged values are recoverable if needed. baseline-condition phases were left untouched (out of scope for that pass) and remain equally unreliable — see §3's caveat before citing baseline beats_completed.
